Identification is informational

Songlark helps you identify birds by sound. It can be wrong, and it never guarantees that a bird is what it says.

Results, including Strong match, Likely, Possible and Unusual here, are suggestions produced by a computer model from the sound it hears. They can be affected by noise, distance, overlapping songs, similar-sounding species, mimics and your region setting. Species names, ranges, photos and descriptions come from third-party sources and may contain errors or be out of date.

Use Songlark for enjoyment and learning. Don't rely on it alone for scientific records, surveys, conservation or legal decisions, or anything where a wrong identification could cause harm. Confirm important sightings by other means.

Using Songlark responsibly

  • Respect birds and their habitats. Don't disturb nesting birds, and be cautious about playing recordings back to birds, which can stress them and is restricted in some places.
  • Follow local laws and the rules of the place you're in, including private land, reserves and parks.
  • Recordings can pick up people's voices. Respect other people's privacy and the recording laws where you are, especially before sharing a recording.
  • Stay aware of your surroundings while using the app outdoors.
