How to listen

  1. Open Songlark and tap Start listening. The first time, iPhone asks for permission to use the microphone; Songlark needs it to hear birds.
  2. Hold your iPhone still, with the bottom edge (where the microphone is) pointing toward the bird.
  3. Watch the sonogram. Songlark listens in five-second windows and starts a new one every two and a half seconds, so a name usually appears a few seconds after the bird sings, pinned to the phrase it belongs to.
  4. Keep listening for a while. A bird that sings several times gives Songlark more to go on than a single call.

Tips for clearer results:

  • Stay quiet and still; footsteps, clothing and talking all show up on the sonogram.
  • Move away from wind, running water, traffic and air conditioning units if you can.
  • Get closer, slowly. Distant birds are faint and easy to confuse.
  • Don't cover the microphone with your fingers or a case.

What the match strengths mean

Songlark never claims to be certain. Each name comes with one of three strengths:

  • Strong matchThe sound fits this species well. Still worth confirming by sight or by listening again before you count it.
  • LikelyA reasonable fit, but another species could sound similar. Look for the bird or wait for it to sing again.
  • PossibleA hint, not an answer. Treat it as a suggestion of what to look for.

Possible matches can be hidden: in Settings, switch off Show possible matches.

The strength describes how well a recording fits, not a promise. A Strong match can still be wrong, especially in noisy places or with birds that imitate others.

Regions and “Unusual here”

Songlark doesn't use your location and never asks for location permission. Instead, you choose a region in Songlark's Settings, under Where you listen. When you first open the app it makes a guess from your iPhone's region setting (Settings › General › Language & Region), which you can change at any time.

The region helps Songlark weigh which birds are likely where you are. A bird that doesn't normally occur in your region needs stronger evidence, and when it is shown it's marked Unusual here. It isn't hidden: vagrants and escaped cage birds do turn up. But an Unusual here result deserves a second look, and it's often a sign the region setting is wrong if you're travelling.

Dawn Chorus tips Pro

Dawn Chorus is an overnight session: start it before bed, and Songlark keeps listening until the time you choose, then shows you every bird it heard and when.

  • Plug in. Listening all night uses battery; keep your iPhone on its charger.
  • Use a windowsill. Birds are much clearer through an open or slightly open window. Keep the phone away from fans, heaters and anything that hums.
  • Check your storage. A session uses about 11 MB per hour, so a full night is roughly 90 MB.
  • Lock the screen. You can lock your iPhone once the session has started; Songlark keeps listening. iPhone shows the microphone indicator while it does.
  • Leave it running. Don't swipe Songlark away in the app switcher, as that stops the session. A phone call or another app using the microphone can also pause it.
  • Pick the end time with sunrise in mind. The busiest singing is usually in the hour or so around sunrise.

Saved recordings

Save a listening session to keep it. Saved recordings can be played back, viewed as a full sonogram, analysed again, and turned into share cards with the sonogram of the song. The free version keeps up to 10 saved recordings; Pro removes the limit. To make room, delete recordings you no longer need.

Importing sound files Pro

You can analyse recordings made with another app or a separate recorder. In Songlark, tap Import a sound file and choose the file from the Files app (including iCloud Drive and other locations Files can reach). The file is copied into Songlark and analysed on your iPhone, just like a live recording.

Exporting

  • Life list: use Export life list to save your list as a CSV file you can open in Numbers, Excel or another spreadsheet app. CSV export is part of Pro.
  • Recordings: from a saved recording, use Export audio or a share card to send it through the share sheet.

Exported and shared files go only where you send them. Songlark doesn't upload them anywhere.

Restoring purchases

If you bought Pro before, or are setting up a new iPhone, open Songlark's Settings and tap Restore purchases (it's also on the Pro screen). Make sure you're signed in to the same Apple Account you used to buy Pro. Restoring is free and doesn't charge you again.

If nothing is restored, check that the subscription is still active (see below) and that you have an internet connection, then try again.

Managing subscriptions

Subscriptions are handled by Apple. To change or cancel one:

  1. Open the Settings app on your iPhone.
  2. Tap your name at the top, then Subscriptions.
  3. Choose Songlark and change or cancel the plan.

Cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the current period to avoid being charged for the next one; you keep Pro until the period ends. Lifetime Pro is a one-time purchase and never renews. For refunds, use Apple's Report a Problem page; Apple handles all payments and refunds.

Frequently asked questions

Does Songlark work offline?
Yes. Identification runs entirely on your iPhone, so it works with no signal, in flight mode or abroad. You only need a connection for optional species photos and for buying or restoring Pro.
How accurate is it?
Songlark doesn't come with accuracy figures, because how well any sound identifier does depends heavily on the recording: distance, noise, how many birds are singing and where you are. Songlark helps you identify birds by sound, and it can be wrong. That's why every result comes with a strength, and why it's worth confirming a bird by sight or by hearing it again before adding it to your life list.
Why did Songlark name the wrong bird?
Common reasons are background noise (wind, traffic, water, voices), a bird that's far away, several birds singing at once, a species with a song very similar to another's, local song dialects, young birds still learning their song, and sounds that aren't birds at all. The wrong region setting can also tip results toward the wrong species. Try getting closer, listening longer, and checking your region.
What about mimics?
Some birds, such as mockingbirds, starlings, many jays and lyrebirds, copy the songs and calls of other species very well. Songlark names what it hears, so a mimic can be reported as the bird it's imitating. A clue is a single bird producing a rapid string of very different phrases. When in doubt, look for the singer.
Does it identify frogs, insects or other animals?
No. Songlark is made for birds, and other sounds aren't named.
Is Songlark recording all the time?
No. It only uses the microphone while you're listening or during a Dawn Chorus session, and iPhone shows the microphone indicator whenever it does.
Where are my recordings stored?
Only on your iPhone. They're never uploaded unless you share or export a file yourself. Deleting Songlark deletes them, so export anything you want to keep first. See the privacy policy.
Can I turn off species photos?
Yes. In Songlark's Settings, switch off Load species photos. Photos come from iNaturalist and are loaded only when you view a species.
What languages are species names in?
Names are available in 12 languages from the IOC World Bird List, shown alongside the English and scientific names.
Is there a free trial of Pro?
No. Live identification, the life list and species pages are free with no time limit, so you can try Songlark properly before deciding.
